I keep coming back to a phrase I use too often and mean too literally: **parallel institutions**.

Not a brand. Not a slide. A working alternative to systems that were never designed for the people who now have to live inside them.

You can feel the gap. There is talent. There is hunger. There is a growing crypto and tech scene. What is scarce is the layer that turns all of that into a place people can actually build a life: education that maps onto jobs, money that holds value through the month, rooms where people keep showing up after the meetup photos are posted.

## What I mean by institution

An institution is a habit with a roof.

It is a Circle that meets whether or not a founder is in town. A payment rail that still works when Twitter is loud and the naira is not. A training series with a next session already dated. A waitlist that is treated as a relationship, not a vanity number.

I have spent years in growth — community scaling, tokenomics, ops, the unglamorous work of making a network actually move. The pattern I trust now is smaller and slower than the one the industry sells: **build the local node first**. Then connect it.

Logos Circle exists because of that bet. Meetup 3.0 was not a panel about “the future of Web3.” It was a room trying to answer a plainer question: how does someone here get a skill, a pathway, and a first real job in this industry?

## The imported playbook problem

Most growth frameworks arrive already finished. They assume density, bandwidth, trust in platforms, and a user who can lose a week to speculation and still eat.

The ground here does not assume that.

So the work looks different:

- Start with a city, not a continent-shaped deck.
- Teach before you extract.
- Put stable value into daily life — bills, sending, saving — before you ask people to believe in a protocol.
- Measure whether the same twelve people come back, not whether a thousand strangers clicked.

This is not anti-ambition. MoyoPay is ambitious. A dollar operating system for Africa is a continental sentence. But the sentence only becomes true if it is true first on the ground: USDT and USDC that can pay a bill, hold a balance, move to family, and feel as ordinary as cash.

## Parallel, not cosplay

There is a temptation to copy the surface of institutions — the meetup, the Discord, the manifesto — and skip the load-bearing parts: custody of trust, boring operations, a calendar that does not slip, a person who answers the message.

Parallel institutions are not a vibe. They are infrastructure with names and addresses.

If this journal has a research question, it is this: **what does it take to stand up those structures here, in public, without waiting for permission from somewhere else?**

I will write the field notes as I find them.
